2. **TODO** — Galileo, from the ESA GSC metadata published in 2017 — dimensions, mass, centre
   of mass, optical coefficients and attitude law.

   *Rule-4 and licence search, 2026-09-24.* The European GNSS Service Centre's metadata page
   prints everything this step needs on the page itself — the reference frame per IOV and FOC, the
   attitude law's equations in the operator's frame and again converted to the GPS/ANTEX
   convention, per-satellite dated mass and centre-of-mass histories, and per-surface optical
   coefficients — retrievable without an account. Its terms authorise reproduction and use
   "provided the source is acknowledged as follows: © EU 2011-2026", so the data is first-party
   and redistributable with that acknowledgement.

   *Ruled the same day.* **The frame:** Galileo's +x points to deep space, not the Sun, so the
   operator's frame is mapped to this tree's one convention by a tested mapping, the published
   conversion checked rather than trusted. **The attitude law** is implemented once and checked
   against both of the operator's printed forms through that mapping — two printed forms are an
   independent check the GPS laws never had — and carries step 6's two guards from the start, time
   running forward and turning the way the nominal turns, each shown firing. **The optical
   letters** are pinned by the operator's own definitions, quoted, and by each material's three
   coefficients summing to one, since `RS14` showed letters differ between sources. **Mass and
   centre of mass** are dated per satellite in the source and the schema holds one value, so the
   library returns a macromodel for a satellite at an epoch — surfaces and optics from its block,
   mass and centre of mass from the entry valid at that epoch — and refuses an epoch outside the
   table; the schema is populated, not extended, and the same shape serves GPS's per-satellite
   masses later (`SPCR-Q-002`).
