1. **TODO** — GPS, from Fliegel & Gallini 1992/1996.

   *Rule-4 and licence search, 2026-09-24.* The IGS satellite metadata SINEX — transmit power and
   mass — is open with attribution, which permits redistribution. Fliegel & Gallini 1992 and 1996
   could not be obtained without an account; the search is recorded. Rodríguez-Solano's 2014
   dissertation (`RS14`) is retrievable and prints box-wing values for Blocks I, IIA and IIR,
   averaged from those two papers; no statement of its redistribution terms was found.

   *Ruled 2026-09-24.* This library was designed as **published values, each with its own
   citation** — the plan names Fliegel & Gallini as this step's source — and §3.6's warning is
   about a hand-built hundred-surface compilation, not about citing a handful of published
   physical parameters, which is how the field cites its models. So `RS14`'s values are used, each
   cited to its own table and through it to the paper it averages, stored in this tree's schema,
   not in `RS14`'s layout, and marked as a secondary source's derivation. Before that, one more
   legitimate route to the primaries — a publisher's own free access to older papers, if it
   exists, and never an account — because a primary, if obtained, is cited instead and `RS14`
   becomes its cross-check. **Block IIF is covered by none of these**, and G01, the load-bearing
   baseline, is a IIF: its sources are searched before anything is built, and if only its
   dimensions are published, its model is this tree's own derivation from them, with optical
   properties from a named analogue marked as assumed. IIR-M uses IIR's values only on a source
   that says its geometry is the same, or marked as assumed where none does.

   *Ruled on the IIF, the same day.* The search found one per-surface source, `RS14`'s own IIF
   table, whose dimensions it takes from "an unpublished document" and whose optical properties are
   a generic assumption; nothing else published breaks the IIF down by surface, and imagery would
   derive less than that table states. So `RS14`'s IIF values are used as its other blocks' are —
   cited per value, marked as a secondary source — with the chain recorded where it ends, the
   optical properties marked as assumed, and the aggregates checked against sources independent of
   it: the mass against the IGS metadata, and the span against any citable published figure. IIR-M
   takes IIR's geometry on Montenbruck 2015's statement that the IIR subtypes differ in their
   antennas, not their buses. **One mapping is pinned before any of it is trusted:** `RS14`'s
   letters for specular and diffuse were read as the reverse of this tree's, and a swap would
   exchange the two for every surface — so the mapping is fixed by the force formula `RS14` itself
   writes with those letters, where the coefficient of 2 cos θ **n̂** is specular and that of
   ⅔ **n̂** diffuse, and by the glass-covered panels' being mostly specular.
