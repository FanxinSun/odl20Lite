6. **TODO** — `thrust-yaw`: antenna thrust and the yaw-attitude laws. Sources: Steigenberger
   2018; Kouba 2009; Montenbruck et al. 2015; the official Galileo, GLONASS and BeiDou
   attitude-law documents. Gate: published yaw angles through noon and midnight turns, per
   constellation.

   *Scope boundary set 2026-09-23.* Step 5 creates the `attitude` module with the **ideal nominal
   yaw-steering law** only — closed-form geometry both force plugins need from the day they exist
   — and that law **refuses** at the degenerate Sun-on-nadir geometry rather than returning a frame
   it cannot define. Everything beyond ideal nominal is this step's: the noon and midnight turns
   the ideal law cannot represent, the constellation-specific laws, and antenna thrust. Step 6
   adds providers to the module step 5 creates; it does not rebuild the nominal law.

   *Scope and order set 2026-09-24, from the rule-4 search and from what the plan's own campaigns
   need.* The frozen GNSS baselines this tree must reproduce are **GPS only** — `G-01`…`G-03` are
   PRNs G01 and G05 in February 2023, and the `B-*` block statistics are GPS IIF, IIR-A/B/M and
   IIIA — and L5's and L8's gates are GPS too. So this step builds **GPS** and gates it; Galileo,
   GLONASS and BeiDou are **carried** as a named follow-on, not dropped, with the sources the
   search found for each recorded (the Galileo operator's metadata page, Dilssner et al. 2011 for
   GLONASS-M, the CSNO standard with a published observed deviation for BeiDou).

   Within GPS, the load-bearing blocks are the ones the baselines use, and **the search's best
   source does not cover them**: Kouba 2009 gives II/IIA and IIR, but IIF launched in 2010 and IIIA
   in 2018. Their laws need their own rule-4 search before the gate is designed.

   *The gate's form, since the sources print formulas and figures rather than numeric yaw tables.*
   Kouba's published noon-turn limits β₀ follow exactly from his published hardware yaw rates:
   the ideal law's peak rate is μ̇/tan β, so tan β₀ = μ̇/R_max, and with GPS's mean motion that
   gives 3.57° to 4.87° for II/IIA's 0.134 to 0.098 °/s and 2.39° for IIR's 0.20 °/s, against the
   printed 3.6–4.9° and 2.4°. The implementation's turn-onset logic must reproduce the published
   β₀ from the published rates — category 1, and independent of transcribing the turn formula.
   Add continuity with the ideal law at each hand-over and |ψ̇| ≤ R_max throughout the turn; and
   where observed attitude is openly published, it is the numeric form this gate's wording asks
   for.

   *Antenna thrust* is a force **law** here — the recoil of the radiated power, P/c along the
   boresight — with P stated in the test, as the L4/L5 decomposition requires. Per-satellite
   transmit powers are L5's population, so Steigenberger 2018 being unreachable does not block
   this step.
