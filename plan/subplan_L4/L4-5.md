5. **TODO** — `srp` and `erp` as force plugins over one photon-pressure kernel. Earth albedo and
   infrared radiation pressure: sources Knocke et al. 1988 and Rodríguez-Solano et al. 2012.

   *Scope amended 2026-09-23, and the reason is a gap in this plan.* Steps 1 and 3 built the
   eclipse shadow function and the SRP force **law**, and no step composed them into a force the
   propagator can use. The only `dyn::Force` in the tree is `Drag`; L4's exit gate — an arc fit
   with this layer's forces — cannot be reached without the SRP plugin. So this step builds both
   plugins over one kernel, SRP first because its single source direction gives closed-form
   checks of the plumbing before ERP's integral over the visible Earth:

   - **The kernel.** `srp_force`'s photon-pressure law with the irradiance a **parameter**, carried
     with its unit in its type (§5 constraint 10), instead of the file-local 1367 W/m². This is
     required for SRP itself, not only for reuse: the Sun's irradiance at the spacecraft varies
     by +3.43 % / −3.26 % over the year, which a constant at 1 AU cannot represent — for the sail,
     a seasonal ±5 × 10⁻⁷ m/s² that a fitted constant C_R cannot absorb over long arcs. `srp_force`'s
     own tests must come out bit-identical after the change. Edited on `DYN-Q-001`'s terms, since
     it touches step 3's closed module.
   - **The SRP plugin.** The Sun's position from `ephemerides`; the irradiance 1367 × (1 au / r)²
     for the spacecraft–Sun distance *r*; the step-1 shadow factor; attitude into the body frame;
     then the kernel. The shadow factor belongs to the SRP plugin and **never** to the shared
     kernel: the Earth does not eclipse its own surface, and a spacecraft in the Earth's shadow
     still receives the Earth's infrared.
   - **The ERP plugin.** The same kernel, summed over the visible cap, with Earth's reflected and
     emitted irradiance per cap element. Albedo and emissivity are **functions of position and
     time** in the interface, constant 0.3 and 0.7 in this step as both accessible sources state,
     so Knocke's zonal and seasonal model — whose paper was not reachable — or a gridded product
     plugs in later without touching the cap integral.

   *Gate — exact identities, not a transcription check.* Neither paper prints a force-level
   number for a stated geometry, and checking an implementation against the thesis's derivation
   would reproduce any error in it. The physics gives two identities independent of both papers:
   the **infrared** from a uniformly emitting Lambertian sphere has net flux exactly *M*(*R*/*r*)²,
   radial, at **every** altitude, so the cap integral on a spherical spacecraft must reproduce
   *C*_R·*A*·*M*(*R*/*r*)²/(*c*·*m*) at LEO as well as GNSS; and the **albedo** of a Lambert sphere
   tends in the far field to (2*A*/3)·*S*·(*R*/*r*)²·Φ(α), Φ(α) = [sin α + (π − α) cos α]/π, whose
   phase integral is exactly 3/2, so it conserves the reflected power *A*·*S*·π*R*². Assert both
   with the **convergence ratio** under refinement (§4 rule 7), characterise the albedo's
   near-field departure rather than asserting it away, and check the visible cap's size — 76.02°,
   37.92 % of the Earth at GNSS altitude.
