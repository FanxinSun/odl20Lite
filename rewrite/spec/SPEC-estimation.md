## 1. Purpose and scope

L7 turns the forces, the integrators, the variational equations (L3) and the measurement models (L6) into **fits**: the whole force model carried by the `dyn::Force` registry (step 1), batch least squares with the normal equations **scaled by default** (step 2), Levenberg–Marquardt (step 3), a priori constraints, isotropic and RTN (step 4), and the **joint covariance** over the state and every registered parameter (step 5). The exit gate (plan §3.8, amended 2026-09-24): *a fit over real data reaches its frozen residual and reports a joint covariance whose correlations are reproduced by finite differences*; cannonball fits with L4's forces reach the `G-*` frozen residuals with their force parameters stated in the test, and a box-wing fit reading L5's library reaches them too.

**Not in scope:** the campaigns (L8); a sail's own macromodel (§3.9); station displacement (outside the MVP, `SPEC-measmod`); the NRLMSISE-00 altitude cutoffs and the attitude discontinuities as integrator events are **carried into** this layer (plan §3.8) and are decided at the step that fits an arc through them.

**Stubs, and when each is written:** §3 (definitions) and §4 (required behaviour) per step, as each step opens, after its rule-4 report is ruled on; §5 (interfaces), §6 (precision), §7 (refusals) likewise; §9 (provenance) at each close. *(v0.2.)* **Step 1 is not specified here.** Ruling R1 (2026-10-06) puts the force-model plugins in a module of their own, `forcemodel` (`SPEC-forcemodel.md`, Spec ID `FMOD`), because the estimator of steps 2 – 5 depends only on `dyn`'s `Force` interface and not on `gravity`, `tides`, `thirdbody` or `relativity`; this document's §3 – §7 are written for steps 2 – 5 when each opens, and its §8.1 is the exit gate's, which spans all five.

## 3. Definitions and conventions

*(Step 2, v0.3.)* The exit gate's statistic is defined in §8.1 because that is where it is registered. What follows is the batch estimator of step 2. **Derivation declaration:** written from `DEM89`, `LAPK`, `NISTL`, `GEO1`, `BRN52`, `MINP` and from no implementation of any estimator; the predecessor's code was not opened.

**3.1 The problem.** A linearised weighted least-squares problem over `n` named **parameters**: `m` scalar **observation rows** `(aₖ, rₖ, σₖ)`, `aₖ ∈ ℝⁿ` the partials of the modelled observation with respect to the parameters at the linearisation point, `rₖ` the **residual** (observed minus modelled) and `σₖ > 0` its standard deviation, in the residual's own units. The estimator returns the **correction** `x̂` that minimises `Ω(x) = Σₖ ((rₖ − aₖᵀx)/σₖ)²` (adding it to the linearisation point is the caller's). Rows are **whitened** on entry: `ãₖ = fl(aₖ/σₖ)` componentwise, `r̃ₖ = fl(rₖ/σₖ)` (σₖ = 1 divides exactly). **The whitened doubles are the data**: every bound below is relative to the exact solution of the problem whose rows are those doubles.

**3.2 The normal equations.** `N = Σₖ ãₖãₖᵀ`, `b = Σₖ ãₖr̃ₖ`, formed as `EST-R-203` states. `D = diag(√Nᵢᵢ)`, `A = D⁻¹ND⁻¹` (unit diagonal, the matrix of the cosines between the whitened columns). **The scaled error** of a computed solution `x̂` against the exact `x = N⁻¹b` is `φ(x̂, x) = ‖D(x̂ − x)‖₂ / ‖Dx̂‖₂`: **invariant under any change of the parameters' units** (a positive diagonal rescaling of the columns changes `D` and `x` inversely), which is the reason it is the measure of this specification and a relative error of the unscaled vector is not (`DEM89`: small components of `x` are computed to the same relative accuracy as large ones when `κ(A)` is moderate).

**3.3 The scaling (the manager's ruling R6).** For a free parameter `i`, `Nᵢᵢ = m·2ᵏ` with `m ∈ [½, 1)` (`frexp`), the **scale exponent** is `eᵢ = ⌊k/2⌋` (`EST-R-201`), `S = diag(2^(−eᵢ))`, `N_s = S N S`, `b_s = S b`, `y = S⁻¹x`, `Q_s = N_s⁻¹`, `Q = N⁻¹ = S Q_s S`. **Scaling by exact powers of two is exact** (barring over- and underflow, `LAPK`): `N_s` carries no rounding the scaling added, and the scaled diagonal `Nᵢᵢ 2^(−2eᵢ)` lies in `[½, 2)`, so `κ₂(N_s) ≤ 4 κ₂(D⁻¹ND⁻¹)` and `N_s` is within `4n` of the best diagonal scaling (`DEM89`'s statement of Van der Sluis). **Consequence, stated so that nobody expects otherwise** (the manager's correction of the premise of this step, 2026-10-06): the scaling cannot change the Cholesky solution — `DEM89` Theorem 2.1 proves that the unscaled factorisation already meets the scaled bound, and with powers of two the two are *bit for bit the same computation* (measured on the eleven NIST datasets, PROVENANCE §40.7). What it changes is **every decision read from the matrix**: the condition number, the observability, the refusals, the per-parameter diagnostics, the correlations, and (step 3) the damping and the tolerances — each of which moves by orders of magnitude with a parameter's unit when read from the unscaled matrix and does not when read from `N_s`.

**3.4 The error model.** `u = 2⁻⁵³`, the unit roundoff of IEEE binary64 under round-to-nearest; `γₖ = ku/(1 − ku)`; `χₙ = 1/(1 − (n+1)u)`. The constants below are `DEM89`'s: Lemma 2.1 (`|(L̂L̂ᵀ − H)ᵢⱼ| ≤ (n+1)u χₙ √(HᵢᵢHⱼⱼ)`), the substitutions' `|δL| ≤ nu|L̂|` that its proof uses, and the proof's final line, `‖D⁻¹FD⁻¹‖₂ ≤ nη + χₙ[(3n²+n)u + n³u²]`, `η` the relative size of the initial error of the matrix factored. For the forming of `N` the elementary dot-product bound `|fl(Σₖ xₖyₖ) − Σₖ xₖyₖ| ≤ γₘ Σₖ|xₖyₖ|` and Cauchy–Schwarz (`Σₖ|ãₖᵢãₖⱼ| ≤ √(NᵢᵢNⱼⱼ)`) give a **componentwise-scaled** error, `|N̂ᵢⱼ − Nᵢⱼ| ≤ γₘ√(NᵢᵢNⱼⱼ)` and `|b̂ᵢ − bᵢ| ≤ γₘ√Nᵢᵢ‖r̃‖₂`, which is exactly the form `DEM89` handles. Hence
- **`Θ_s(n) = χₙ [ (3n² + n) u + n³u² ]`** — the factorisation and the two substitutions, in the scaled 2-norm;
- **`Θ_N(n, m) = n γₘ (1 + γₘ) + Θ_s(n)`** — plus the forming of `N`; the test-level versions add `n·η_rep`, `η_rep` the relative representation error of decimal data rounded to double (`2.01 u`, §6.1).
`n` is the number of FREE parameters and `m` the number of rows. **The solve's certified relative error** is `B` of `EST-R-206`.

**3.5 What a solution reports** (`EST-R-207`, `-210`): the condition of `N_s` (an estimate from below and a bound from above), each parameter's own `κᵢ = Nᵢᵢ(N⁻¹)ᵢᵢ = (N_s)ᵢᵢ(Q_s)ᵢᵢ` (unit-free: `GEO1` §10.5), the **weakest direction**, `B`, the residual sum of squares `Ω̂`, the degrees of freedom `ν = m − n_free`, the variance of unit weight `σ̂₀² = Ω̂/ν`, and the covariance **both ways** — formal (`σ₀ = 1`: the weights as stated) and scaled (`σ̂₀²`) — never one for the other (`BRN52` §7.2).

**3.6 The nonlinear problem (step 3, v0.4).** **Derivation declaration:** written from `DTU`, `NIE99`, `MINP` and `NISTN`, and from no implementation of any least-squares code (the MINPACK listings were never opened). The module minimises `F(x) = ½ Σₖ rₖ(x)²`, `rₖ = (yₖ − fₖ(x))/σₖ` **whitened by the caller** (a weight is never defaulted, as in `EST-R-202`); `A = ∂f/∂x` divided row by row by `σₖ` is the Jacobian **of the model**, so that the Gauss–Newton step solves `N h = b` with `N = AᵀA` and `b = Aᵀr` and moves `x` towards the data (the sign convention of §3.1: *residual = observed − modelled*). The model is a callable returning, at a point `x`, the residuals, the Jacobian (only when asked) and optionally the objective (an objective returned by the model replaces `½Σr²`: step 4's prior is part of the objective, and the gain ratio is taken on the **whole** objective), **or a refusal**. **The damped system, in the scaled variables:** at each iterate the scale exponents `eᵢ` of `EST-R-201` are recomputed from the diagonal of that iterate's `N`; the damped step solves `(N + μW)h = b` with `W = diag(2^(2eᵢ))`, which is `(N_s + μI)y = b_s` in the scaled variables, **through step 2's `from_normal_system`**: the scaled path stays the **only** path and step 3 writes no factorisation. **The gain ratio** is `ρ = (F(x) − F(x+h)) / L`, `L = ½(hᵀb + μ hᵀWh)`. **The gradient cosine** is `c = maxᵢ |cos(r, aᵢ)|` over the columns `aᵢ` of `A`: a dimensionless measure of how far `Aᵀr` is from zero, which does not change when the residuals or a parameter's unit are rescaled. **A statement that holds throughout:** every decision step 3 reads from the matrix (the scale, the damping, the tests) is made on the scaled quantities and is dimensionless (§3.3's consequence).

**3.7 The a priori constraint (step 4, v0.5, 2026-11-27).** **Derivation declaration:** written from `BRN52` §7.5, `GEO1` §10.1 – 10.2 and `FRAME-R-040` – `-044`, read from the pinned text layers, and from no implementation of any constraint (none of step 4 exists; the manager's rulings R13 and R14 of 2026-10-06 are in `plan/subplan_L7/L7-4.md`). **A constraint is information about the parameters that the data did not give**, entered as a Gaussian prior with centre `x_A` and information matrix `Λ` (`Λ = V_A⁻¹` of `GEO1`; `HᵀP_hH` of `BRN52`, with `H` a selection of the parameters). It is read, as `BRN52` reads it, as **pseudo-observations**: `r` rows `(L, d)` with `LᵀL = Λ`, which enter `N` and `b` exactly as data rows do (`EST-R-203`) and therefore **before the scaling of `EST-R-201`** — the scale exponents are taken from the diagonal of the **total** `N = Σ ãₖãₖᵀ + Λ`, so that a tight constraint is scaled away instead of ruining the conditioning (§3.3: the scaling is exact and decides only what is read from the matrix).

**Variables.** In the batch problem of §3.1 the unknown is the correction `c` to a linearisation point `x_lin`; the prior's **departure** is `d = fl(x_A − x_lin)` (the prior's residual at the linearisation point: `GEO1`'s `x_A − x̂⁽ⁿ⁾`). **The objective the estimator minimises is**

**`F_tot(c) = ½‖r̃ − Ac‖² + ½ (c − d)ᵀ Λ (c − d)`,**

the data's `½Ω` plus the prior's quadratic form in the **absolute** variable (`x = x_lin + c`: `(x − x_A) = (c − d)`). Its normal equations are `(N + Λ)c = b + Λd`, the sum `BRN52` prints with `HᵀP_hh` equal to `Λd` — and `GEO1`'s iteration, the term `Σ_A⁻¹(x_A − x̂⁽ⁿ⁾)` being `Λd` at the iterate `x̂⁽ⁿ⁾ = x_lin`. **In the nonlinear problem of §3.6**, at the current iterate `x` the model's rows are the data's and the prior's rows are `(L, L(x_A − x))`: the gradient right-hand side is `b_tot = b_data + Λ(x_A − x)` and **the objective the gain ratio `ρ` sees is the whole `F_tot(x) = ½‖r(x)‖² + ½(x − x_A)ᵀΛ(x − x_A)`**, so that a step which lowers the data's misfit and raises the prior's by more is *rejected*.

**The two forms of `Λ`.**
- **Isotropic.** Over the named parameters of a block, `Λ = diag(σᵢ⁻²)`; **`I/σ²`** is the case of one `σ` for the block. Row `i` of `L` is `eᵢ/σᵢ`.
- **RTN.** A block is **three named parameters that are the inertial Cartesian components of one vector** — a position, a velocity, or any vector-valued force parameter — **each in its own block and its own fixed basis**. With `B` the 3 × 3 matrix `rtn_basis(r_A, v_A)` returns (**its rows are `ê_R`, `ê_T`, `ê_N`: `FRAME-R-040`; it maps inertial components to RTN components**),
  **`Λ_block = Bᵀ diag(σ_R⁻², σ_T⁻², σ_N⁻²) B`**, i.e. `(c − d)ᵀΛ(c − d) = Σ_k ((B(c − d))_k / σ_k)²` over `k = R, T, N`. *(The ruled formula is written `R diag(σ⁻²) Rᵀ` with `R` the matrix whose **columns** are the axes: `R = Bᵀ`. The two are the same matrix; **a transposed basis is the error this note exists to prevent**, and `EST-A-403` and `EST-A-407` each carry a control for it.)* Row `k` of `L` is `(1/σ_k)·B_k` on the block's three parameters. **`B` is computed once, from the a priori state `(r_A, v_A)`, and held fixed through the iteration**: it is a statement about the prior (where its axes lie), not a nonlinear constraint, so no derivative of `B` enters a Jacobian. `ê_T` is **not** the velocity direction (`FRAME-R-042`) and a velocity block uses the position's basis frozen at the epoch (`FRAME-R-043`); a rectilinear or zero state has no basis and `rtn_basis` refuses it (`FRAME-R-044`, `FRAME-F-004`): the caller has then **no prior to give**.
- **A hard fix is elimination (`EST-R-209`), never `σ → 0`.** `σ → 0` is accepted, to the range of `EST-F-402`, and `EST-A-402` measures how nearly it reproduces the elimination; it is **not** how a parameter is fixed.

**What a prior changes in what a solution reports** (`EST-R-403`): the residual sum of squares has two parts, the data's `Ω̂_data = Σₖ ρ̂ₖ²` and the prior's `Ω̂_prior = Σ (Lĉ − d_s)ₖ²` (its rows' residuals), `Ω̂_tot` their sum; the prior rows are counted as observations, `ν = m + r − n_free`, `r` the number of prior rows. **This counting is the definition adopted here and `EST-Q-007` asks the manager to rule on it**: `BRN52` §7.5 prints the NEQ and calls the constraints pseudo-observations but, in the sections searched (`PROVENANCE.md` §40.10), prints a degrees-of-freedom rule for the data alone (`f = n − u`, §7.2). The posterior cofactor is `Q_post = (N + Λ)⁻¹`, which is what the solve's cofactor already is (`EST-R-205`).

## 4. Required behaviour

*(Written per step; step 2's requirements are `EST-R-201` – `EST-R-213`, then the exit gate's.)*

**Step 2 — the batch estimator, the normal equations scaled by default.**

- **EST-R-201.** **The scale rule, and no path without it.** For a free parameter whose diagonal `d = N̂ᵢᵢ` is finite and lies in `[2⁻¹⁰⁰⁰, 2¹⁰⁰⁰]`, write `d = m·2ᵏ` with `m ∈ [½, 1)` (`std::frexp`); **the scale exponent is `e = ⌊k/2⌋`** (an integer floor division, no logarithm and no tie to break); the scaled diagonal `d·2^(−2e)` lies in `[½, 2)`. `N_s,ᵢⱼ = ldexp(N̂ᵢⱼ, −(eᵢ+eⱼ))`, `b_s,ᵢ = ldexp(b̂ᵢ, −eᵢ)`, the solution `xᵢ = ldexp(yᵢ, −eᵢ)` and the cofactor `Qᵢⱼ = ldexp(Q_s,ᵢⱼ, −(eᵢ+eⱼ))` — **all exact**. The interface offers **no option and no function that factors or solves an unscaled matrix**: unscaled quantities are outputs only. A diagonal `≤ 0` is `EST-F-201`; one non-finite or outside the range, `EST-F-206`.
- **EST-R-202.** **Rows.** `add(row, residual, sigma)` requires `row.size() = n`, every value finite and `sigma` finite and `> 0` (`EST-F-204`; **a weight is never defaulted**: the caller states `σ`, even 1). The row and the residual are whitened by division (`fl(aₖ/σₖ)`, `fl(rₖ/σₖ)`) and stored in the order added.
- **EST-R-203.** **The forming.** `N̂ᵢⱼ` (`i ≥ j`) is the plain double sum `(((0 + p₁) + p₂) + …) + p_m`, `pₖ = fl(ãₖᵢ ãₖⱼ)`, **rows in the order added**; the upper triangle is its mirror image; `b̂ᵢ` likewise with `pₖ = fl(ãₖᵢ r̃ₖ)`. The module is built with fused multiply-add contraction **off**, so that this order and these roundings are the registered ones; the bounds of §3.4 hold with or without contraction.
- **EST-R-204.** **The factorisation and the solves** are `DEM89`'s Algorithm 2.1 on the lower triangle of `N_s` and the two substitutions, in plain double: for `i = 0 … n_f−1`, `t = N_s,ᵢᵢ` minus `Σ_{k<i} L²ᵢₖ` **by successive subtraction in the order `k = 0 … i−1`**; **if `!(t > 0)` the factorisation breaks down at `i`** (`EST-F-202`); `Lᵢᵢ = √t`; for `j > i`, `Lⱼᵢ = (N_s,ⱼᵢ minus Σ_{k<i} LⱼₖLᵢₖ by successive subtraction)/Lᵢᵢ`. Forward substitution `Lz = b_s` ascending, back substitution `Lᵀy = z` descending, each by successive subtraction. Extended precision is **not** used in production.
- **EST-R-205.** **The solution and the cofactor.** `x` by the unscaling of `y`. The cofactor by `n_f` solves `N_s q = eⱼ` with the same factor; `Q_s,ᵢⱼ = qⱼ[i]` for `i ≥ j` and the upper triangle its mirror image, so **`Q` is exactly symmetric**. An eliminated parameter has no uncertainty in this model: its row and column of `Q` are exactly 0.
- **EST-R-206.** **What the solve certifies, and the refusal that follows from it.** Let `q₁ = ‖Q̂_s‖₁` (the largest column sum of absolute values of the computed scaled cofactor), `X = (Σᵢ N_s,ᵢᵢ yᵢ²)^½ = ‖Dx̂‖₂`, `ρ̄ = ‖r̃′‖₂` (the residual vector that formed `b̂`, after any elimination), `ρ̄₀ = ‖r̃‖₂` (before), and `μ_E = ‖(Σ_e |ãₖₑ||vₑ|)ₖ‖₂` (0 when nothing is eliminated). **The certified scaled relative error is**
  **`B = 4 q₁ [ Θ_N(n_f, m) + √n_f (γₘ ρ̄ + γ_{2n_e}(ρ̄₀ + μ_E)) / X ]`** — in system mode (`EST-R-213`) the forming terms are absent and `Θ_N` is replaced by `Θ_s(n_f)`; `B = 0` when `b̂ = 0` (then `x̂ = 0` exactly). *Derivation (informative):* `x̂ − x = N⁻¹(Δb − ΔN x̂)` is an identity for the computed `x̂`; `‖D⁻¹ΔND⁻¹‖₂ ≤ Θ_N` and `‖D⁻¹Δb‖₂ ≤ √n_f(…)` by §3.4; `D(x̂ − x) = A⁻¹D⁻¹(Δb − ΔNx̂)`; `‖A⁻¹‖₂ ≤ 2‖N_s⁻¹‖₂` because `N_s` has its diagonal below 2, and `‖N_s⁻¹‖₂ ≤ 2‖Q̂_s‖₂ ≤ 2q₁` whenever `B < ½` (the computed inverse is the inverse of a matrix within `2Θ_N` of `N_s`, and `2Θ_N q₁ < ¼`). **If `B ≥ ½`, or if `Q̂_s` is not finite, the solve certifies not even one digit and is REFUSED (`EST-F-203`)** — a bound, not a number anyone chose.
- **EST-R-207.** **The condition report.** `κ_est = λ_N λ_Q`, the Rayleigh quotients after **100 power iterations** on `N_s` and on `Q_s`, each started from `(1, …, 1)/√n_f` (so `κ_est ≤ κ₂(N_s)`); `κ_up = ‖N_s‖₁ ‖Q_s‖₁` (so `κ₂(N_s) ≤ κ_up`); **`κᵢ = (N_s)ᵢᵢ (Q_s)ᵢᵢ`** for every free parameter (`NaN` for an eliminated one); **the weakest direction** = the unit vector of the `Q_s` iteration, sign fixed so that its largest-magnitude component is positive, in the scaled variables, 0 for eliminated parameters. These are reported with every solution and with `EST-F-203`.
- **EST-R-208.** **Every refusal names the parameter and why.** `EST-F-201` names every parameter with no information. `EST-F-202` names the parameter `j` at which the factorisation broke down, the pivot `t`, and **the dependency**: with `z` the solution of `N_s[0:j−1, 0:j−1] z = N_s[0:j−1, j]` (the same successive-subtraction substitutions on the factor built so far), `N_s[:, j] ≈ Σ_{k<j} z_k N_s[:, k]` — the three parameters of largest `|z_k|` by name with their `z_k`; the weakest direction reported is `(−z, 1)` normalised, in the scaled variables, 0 for the parameters after `j`. `EST-F-203` carries `B`, `κ_est`, `κ_up`, the three largest `κᵢ` by name and the three largest members of the weakest direction by name.
- **EST-R-209.** **The one hatch: elimination of named parameters at stated values, logged.** `SolveOptions::eliminate` lists parameters by name with a value; each name must be known and listed once, each value finite, at least one parameter must remain and the estimator must hold the rows (`EST-F-204`). The elimination is at the row level: `r̃′ₖ = r̃ₖ − ãₖ,e₁ v₁ − ãₖ,e₂ v₂ − …`, left to right in ascending parameter index, each product and each difference rounded; the normal equations are those of the free parameters and `r̃′`; the solution holds every eliminated parameter at its stated value; `ν = m − n_free`; the provenance records them (`EST-R-212`). This is **the operation a hard constraint is** (step 4), and it removes a parameter from the model: it never returns numbers for combinations the data cannot see.
- **EST-R-210.** **The residual, the degrees of freedom and the covariance.** `ρ̂ₖ = fl(r̃ₖ − sₖ)`, `sₖ = Σᵢ ãₖᵢ xᵢ` over **all** parameters in index order (the eliminated at their values) by successive addition; `Ω̂ = Σₖ ρ̂ₖ²` by successive addition; `ν = m − n_free`; `σ̂₀² = Ω̂/ν` for `ν > 0`. **Formal covariance** `= Q` (`σ₀ = 1`, the weights as stated); **scaled covariance** `= σ̂₀² Q`; standard deviations `√Qᵢᵢ` and `σ̂₀√Qᵢᵢ`; **correlation** `ρᵢⱼ = Q_s,ᵢⱼ / √(Q_s,ᵢᵢ Q_s,ⱼⱼ)` computed from the SCALED cofactor (so it is unit-free by construction), clamped to `[−1, 1]`, `ρᵢᵢ = 1`. `σ̂₀² = 0` (a perfect fit) gives an exactly zero scaled covariance while the formal one stays defined. The two covariances are never substituted for each other.
- **EST-R-211.** **Unit invariance, exactly.** If every column `j` of the rows is multiplied by `2^(pⱼ)` (the parameter `xⱼ` is then `2^(−pⱼ)` of what it was), then — barring over- and underflow — the scale exponents move by `pⱼ`, and **`N_s`, `b_s`, the factor, `y`, `Q_s`, `κ_est`, `κ_up`, the `κᵢ`, the weakest direction, the correlations, `B`, `Ω̂`, `σ̂₀²` and every refusal are bit-identical**; `xⱼ` and the cofactor scale by exactly `2^(−pⱼ)` and `2^(−pᵢ−pⱼ)`. (Every operation commutes with exact power-of-two scalings; `frexp`'s exponent moves by `2pⱼ` and `⌊k/2⌋` by `pⱼ`.) For a rescaling that is not a power of two the solutions agree within the sum of the two certified errors (`EST-A-209`).
- **EST-R-212.** **Provenance.** Every solution carries a string in the tree's `key=value;` form, in this key order: `estimation=batch-normal-equations;scaling=powers-of-two;parameters=<n>;free=<n_free>;observations=<m>;eliminated=<name=value,…|none>;exponents=<e₀,e₁,…>;` — the values printed `%.17g`, the exponents those of the free parameters in order.
- **EST-R-213.** **System mode.** `from_normal_system(names, N, b, observations, residual_square_sum)` takes an accumulated normal system (the lower triangle of `N` is read) for callers who hold one — a combination of campaigns, step 4's priors, a published example. The forming terms of `EST-R-206` are absent (the system is exact as given), `Ω̂ = Σr̃² − bᵀx̂` **only when the caller supplied the residual square sum** (a difference of two large numbers, documented as such), and elimination is refused (`EST-F-204`: it needs the rows).
**Step 3 — Levenberg–Marquardt, in the scaled variables** *(v0.4, 2026-11-25; module `estimation`, header `odl/estimation/levenberg_marquardt.hpp`, source `src/levenberg_marquardt.cpp`; the manager's rulings of 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-31, the user's decisions of 2026-11-25 — `plan/subplan_L7/L7-3.md`)*

- **EST-R-301.** **The algorithm.** `DTU`'s Algorithm 3.16 in the scaled variables of §3.6: at every iterate the exponents are recomputed from `N`'s diagonal, the damped system `(N + μW)h = b` is built as `(N_s + μI)y = b_s` and solved by `NormalEquations::from_normal_system`; **no factorisation is written in step 3 and no path solves an unscaled system.** The starting damping is `μ₀ = τ · maxᵢ (N_s)ᵢᵢ` (the scaled diagonal lies in `[½, 2)`, so `τ` multiplies a number of order one).
- **EST-R-302.** **The gain ratio and the damping update.** `ρ = (F(x) − F(x+h)) / L`, `L = ½(hᵀb + μ hᵀWh)`, on the objective the model carries. **A step is taken exactly when `ρ > 0`** (a gain ratio in `(0, ½]` is accepted: `EST-A-311`'s guard of the mutant `ρ > ½`). On acceptance `μ ← μ · max(⅓, 1 − (2ρ − 1)³)` and `ν ← 2` (`DTU` (2.21)); on rejection `μ ← μ · ν` and `ν ← 2ν`, so **consecutive rejections multiply `μ` by 2, 4, 8, …** exactly (`EST-A-313`'s guard of the mutant `ν ← ν`).
- **EST-R-303.** **A failed trial is a rejected step.** A trial the model refuses, a non-finite objective, residual or Jacobian, and a damped system that step 2 refuses all **raise `μ` and the iteration goes on**; none aborts the run. (The model's refusal of a trial is how L6's observability limit — a leg below 3° of elevation, `MEAS-F-010` — reaches the optimiser: Gauss–Newton has no defence against it and the damped method does: §8.3's sparse-arc rows.)
- **EST-R-304.** **The stopping tests are dimensionless.** At every iterate, **in this order**: (a) **the zero-residual test**, `F ≤ residual_fraction · F(x₀)` (10⁻²⁴): `converged_residual` (for the consistent problems whose gradient cosine is indeterminate); (b) **the gradient test**, `c ≤ gradient_cosine` (10⁻¹⁰): `converged_gradient`; (c) **the limit**, `max_iterations` passes (1000, every pass counted, accepted or rejected): `max_iterations`. At every pass, on the damped step **before** its trial: (d) **the step test on the scaled quantities**, `‖h_s‖ ≤ ε₂(‖x_s‖ + ε₂)` with `ε₂ = step_tolerance` (10⁻¹²): `converged_step` **if** `c ≤ stalled_cosine` (10⁻⁴), otherwise **`stalled`** (the step test takes the step that was *not* made: the returned iterate is the last accepted one). Rescaling the residuals or a parameter's unit by powers of two changes none of them (`EST-R-308`).
- **EST-R-305.** **A result carries a status, never a bare "converged"**, with the message naming what ended the run and the numbers: `converged_gradient`, `converged_residual`, `converged_step` (the step test held and the gradient cosine is not above `stalled_cosine`), `stalled` (the step test held while the gradient cosine **is** above it: *the iteration stopped moving without having arrived*, in the test's own sense), `max_iterations`, `refused` (`EST-R-307`). **`stalled` at `χ²/obs ≈ 1` on noisy data is the truthful end state** (§8.3's `ARC-X-014` row: the objective sits on the model's own evaluation-noise floor there, and the user's decision of 2026-11-25 keeps the rule unchanged). The result also carries the last accepted iterate `x`, `F`, the cosine, the pass, accepted and rejected counts, the final `μ`, one history record per pass (`k`, `F` before the trial, the cosine, `μ` used, `ρ` — NaN for a trial that could not be evaluated —, the scaled step, whether accepted), `x` after each accepted step, and **the undamped solve at the last iterate** (step 2's `Solution`: the cofactor, the condition report, `σ̂₀²`) **when step 2 accepts the system**.
- **EST-R-306.** **The defaults** (`LmOptions`), each with where it comes from: `tau = 10⁻³` (registered before the first run; **the candidate τ = 1 lost the held-out test** of `EST-A-331` and the default stays); `gradient_cosine = 10⁻¹⁰`; `residual_fraction = 10⁻²⁴` (**first registered 10⁻³⁰, which sits at the rounding floor of `F` and reported success as `stalled`; changed by the manager's ruling of 2026-10-31, registered before the re-run**); `step_tolerance = 10⁻¹²`; `stalled_cosine = 10⁻⁴`; `max_iterations = 1000`; `mu_max = 10¹⁶`. **No default was tuned to a result seen** (§8.3's rows state what each decision rested on).
- **EST-R-307.** **`refused`** means: the model refused **the starting point** (`EST-F-301`; no pass was made); or `μ` exceeded `mu_max` (`EST-F-302`); or a stop (the step test or the limit) came **while the latest trial could not be evaluated** (`EST-F-303`) — the run never reports arrival at a point where nothing was evaluated. *(The narrowing of `refused` to the third case alone, and a step test in the Hessian's metric, were proposed in commit `2033ad8` of `plan/subplan_L7/L7-3.md`, suspended on 2026-10-31 and **retired on 2026-11-25 without ever being built** (Amendment 9): `refused` and the stopping rule stand as stated here.)*
- **EST-R-308.** **Invariance under power-of-two rescaling, exactly.** If the residuals are multiplied by `2^q` and the parameters' units by `2^(pⱼ)`, the iterates (rescaled), the statuses, the damping and every decision are **bit-identical** (`MINP` §2.5's definition of scale invariance). **No such claim is made for any other change of variables**: Meyer's two formulations (`DTU` Example 3.19) are *not* related by a power of two and their iterates differ (up to 0.1685 relative over 152 common steps, `EST-A-320`).
- **EST-R-309.** **Stated limits, not claims.** (i) The module makes **no claim that the scaled algorithm is more robust than `DTU`'s unscaled one**: it reaches NIST's `F` from 46 of 54 starts against the unscaled control's 51 (§8.3), and its advantage is `EST-R-308`, not reach; (ii) **no claim about Levenberg–Marquardt's robustness is made from the dense sail arc**, on which plain Gauss–Newton also reaches the truth from every evaluable start: that problem is close to linear (`ARC-L-001`, §8.3); the sparse arc is where the damped method has its evidence; (iii) the integrator's evaluation noise sets a floor under `F` and the gradient cosine of a model evaluated through `dyn::propagate` (`ARC-X-014`): a fit cannot be driven below it and the status says so (`EST-R-305`).
**Step 4 — the a priori constraints, isotropic and RTN** *(v0.5, 2026-11-27, **registered before any line of step 4 exists**; module `estimation`, namespace `odl::estimation`, headers `odl/estimation/prior.hpp` (new) and the existing `normal_equations.hpp`; `core`-only; the manager's rulings R13 and R14 of 2026-10-06 — `plan/subplan_L7/L7-4.md`)*

- **EST-R-401.** **A prior block, and what is validated before anything is added.** A block names its parameters and gives, for each, a centre `x_A` and a standard deviation; the **isotropic** block takes any number of parameters, each with its own `σᵢ`; the **RTN** block takes **exactly three** parameters (the inertial `x, y, z` components of one vector, in that order), a 3 × 3 basis in the layout of `FRAME-R-040` (**row-major, rows `ê_R`, `ê_T`, `ê_N`**) and `(σ_R, σ_T, σ_N)`. Validation, **all of it before any row is formed** and with the offending name in the diagnostic (`EST-F-401` – `EST-F-406`): every name known to the estimator and listed once in its block; **no parameter in two blocks** (no silent combination: a caller who wants two pieces of information about one parameter builds one block); every centre and `σ` finite and `σ` in `[2⁻⁵⁰⁰, 2⁵⁰⁰]` (so that `σ⁻²` and every whitened entry stay normal doubles — a range, not a tuned floor); the basis finite and orthonormal, `max |B Bᵀ − I| ≤ 10⁻¹²`; **no parameter that is both constrained and eliminated** (`EST-R-209`).
- **EST-R-402.** **The prior's rows, formed with the data's, before the scaling.** An isotropic block adds, per parameter `i`, the whitened row `(eᵢ/σᵢ)` with residual `d̃ᵢ = fl(dᵢ/σᵢ)`; an RTN block adds three rows, `k = R, T, N`, with entries `fl(B_kj/σ_k)` on its three parameters and residual `fl(fl(Σ_j B_kj dⱼ)/σ_k)`, `Σ_j` in `j = x, y, z` by successive addition; where `d = fl(x_A − x_lin)` is taken at the linearisation point the **caller states with the block** (`add_prior(block, x_lin)`). The rows are appended **after the data's, in the order the blocks were added**, and are formed into `N̂` and `b̂` by the rule of `EST-R-203` (so `Λ` is **exactly symmetric by construction**, the upper triangle the mirror of the lower, and a `NormalEquations` that has no prior is **bit-identical to step 2's**, every field and the provenance string). The exponents of `EST-R-201` are then taken from the diagonal of the **total** `N̂`; **no path adds a prior after the scaling, and none solves without it.**
- **EST-R-403.** **What the solution reports, and the counts.** `Solution` gains the number of prior rows `r`, **`Ω̂_data`, `Ω̂_prior` and `Ω̂_tot = Ω̂_data + Ω̂_prior`** (the data's from the data rows' residuals `ρ̂ₖ` as in `EST-R-210`, the prior's from the prior rows' residuals `fl(d̃ₖ − Σⱼ L̃ₖⱼ xⱼ)`, both by successive addition), `ν = m + r − n_free` (`EST-Q-007`) and `σ̂₀² = Ω̂_tot/ν` for `ν > 0`; the formal covariance `Q = (N + Λ)⁻¹` (**the posterior cofactor**) and the scaled one `σ̂₀²Q` are as in `EST-R-210`. **The provenance string of `EST-R-212` gets, after `exponents=…;` and only when at least one block was added, the keys `priors=<number of blocks>;prior_rows=<r>;`** — a solution with no prior carries the step-2 string byte for byte. The step-2 and step-3 tests are unchanged.
- **EST-R-404.** **The damped method sees the whole objective.** `with_priors(model, blocks)` wraps an `LmModel` and returns an `LmModel` whose evaluation at `x` is the model's, **with the prior's rows appended** — residual `L(x_A − x)` and Jacobian `L`, `m + r` rows — so that `F_tot(x) = ½‖r(x)‖² + ½(x − x_A)ᵀΛ(x − x_A)` is the `½Σr²` the gain ratio of `EST-R-302` is taken on, the gradient is `b_tot = b_data + Λ(x_A − x)`, and **`levenberg_marquardt.cpp` is not changed** (the prior reaches it as rows; an objective carried by the model, `LmEvaluation::objective`, is not needed and **is not used**: a model that already carries one is refused, `EST-F-404`, because the prior's half could not be added to it without a second, unregistered convention). Priors and the model share the parameter names of the call; a name the prior holds that the call does not is `EST-F-401`.
- **EST-R-405.** **Exact invariance, and the hard fix.** If a column `j` of the data rows is multiplied by `2^(pⱼ)` — and **the prior's `σⱼ` and centre by the same `2^(pⱼ)`** for an isotropic parameter, or **all three of an RTN block's `σ_k` and `d` by one common `2^p`** (the basis `B` is then unchanged: a different power on one component of a block would rotate the ellipsoid and is **not** a power-of-two rescaling) — then every quantity listed in `EST-R-211` **and `Λ`, the prior rows' sums, `Ω̂_prior`, `Ω̂_tot`, `ν` and the new provenance keys** are bit-identical or scale by exactly the power of two, in the batch solve and, for `EST-R-308`, in every pass of the damped method. **A hard fix is the elimination of `EST-R-209`**: nothing in this step turns `σ → 0` into a hatch, there is no `σ` that means "fixed", and the range of `EST-R-401` is the only limit (`EST-A-402` measures how far the small-`σ` limit agrees with the elimination; it is the evidence that the information form is safe, not an invitation).
- **EST-R-406.** **Observability.** The refusals of step 2 are unchanged and a prior does not hide them: a parameter that no row observes **and that no block constrains** is `EST-F-201` (named), exactly as without step 4; **a parameter that only a block constrains is observable** (its diagonal is `Λᵢᵢ > 0`: `GEO1` §10.2, *"the existence of the a priori information alone guarantees that the inverse of A exists"*) and the solve returns the prior's centre for it, to the certified error `B`.
- **EST-R-407.** **Stated limits, not claims.** (i) The module makes **no claim that a constraint improves an estimate**: it **adds information**, and a wrong centre with a tight `σ` is returned as the answer (that is the bias of `EST-A-407`, registered as a number); (ii) **the evidence of step 4 is identities and one constructed comparison**, because neither `BRN52` nor `GEO1` prints a numerical example of a constraint: the comparison is a **linear Gaussian construction with a known truth** and its predicted values are closed forms; **it makes no claim about any real orbit, any real tracking network or any value of `σ_T` that a user should choose**; (iii) the noise in `EST-A-407`'s replicates is **reported**, and gates nothing (`EST-A-407`: the RTN bias is below the replicates' standard error, and the table says so).
- **EST-R-900.** *(registered 2026-10-06, before any fit exists.)* The exit gate's fit residual is the statistic **S** of §8.1, computed from the fit's own output by the formula there; a fit **reaches** its frozen residual exactly when `S ≤ F` for its row of §8.1, to the digits `oracle/cases.tsv` prints. The test **prints** `S`, `N`, the per-component and radial/along/cross values and the largest epoch residual beside the frozen value, whichever way the comparison falls.

### 8.1 The exit gate: what "reaches its frozen residual" means — **registered 2026-10-06, before any fit runs** (plan §4 rule 3)

**The frozen rows, as `oracle/cases.tsv` prints them:** `G-01` **0.0645 m** (`validate_sp3.sh 2246 20230220000 G01`), `G-02` **0.0573 m** (`2246 20230230000 G01`), `G-03` **0.1870 m** (`2246 20230220000 G05`), each "GNSS 7-parameter fit residual RMS"; `oracle/ORACLE.md` §6 records them as **cannonball** fits whose seventh parameter is the SRP scale, against IGS final orbits. The stamps are read as the IGS long-name product stamp `YYYY DDD 0000` (GPS week 2246 begins 2023-01-22, day 22): **G01 on 2023-01-22 and on 2023-01-23, G05 on 2023-01-22**.

**The statistic.** For a fit of a 24-hour arc to the IGS file's positions `pᵢ` of the satellite (the file's own epochs `tᵢ` inside the arc, **`N` of them — the 15-minute grid, 96 epochs**; `pᵢ` in the ITRS in metres) with the fitted model's positions `qᵢ(θ̂)` at the same epochs, in the ITRS (the model's GCRS position rotated by the same chain as everywhere in this tree):

> **S = √( (1/N) Σᵢ₌₁..N ‖qᵢ(θ̂) − pᵢ‖² )**, in metres.

**Which components:** all three, as the 3-D Euclidean norm of the position difference at each epoch. **Which epochs:** every epoch of the file's grid inside the arc; **none rejected**, none skipped (an epoch the file marks absent or bad refuses the test rather than being dropped). **Which denominator:** `N`, the number of epochs — **not** `N − 7` and **not** `3N`; no degrees-of-freedom correction. *[v0.2, 2026-10-06, the first text kept: whether the frozen number's own denominator is `N` or `N − 7` is not known and cannot be told from its printed output; `S` with `N − 7` is reported beside `S` with `N`, and the amendment below governs the outcome.]* Reported beside `S`, never instead of it: `S/√3` (the per-component reading), the radial, along-track and cross-track RMS (each `√(mean of that component²)`), the largest epoch residual, `N`, and the fitted parameters.

**How the frozen numbers are used.** They **rank last** (plan §4 rule 2): they are the predecessor's output and detect only gross error — a sign, an axis, a factor of two. The weight of the exit gate rests on its second clause, the joint covariance's correlations reproduced by finite differences, whose checks are independent of the predecessor. **The registered pass rule: a fit reaches its frozen residual iff `S ≤ F`, `F` the row's printed value** — `0.0645`, `0.0573`, `0.1870` — for the cannonball fit, and the same rule for the box-wing fit reading L5's library.

**The prediction, written with its reason before any fit.** `S/F` between **0.6 and 1.1** for each of the three rows: the cannonball physics is that of L4's `Srp`/ECOM, with L2's field, third bodies, tides and relativity, which the predecessor's fit may not have carried in full; a ratio above 1.1 is a finding, brought to the manager unchanged, not tuned away. (`EST-A-900` states the prediction and the comparison separately; the prediction is not a criterion.)

**Two honest limits, registered with the rule.** (1) **The frozen number's own formula is not recorded anywhere in this tree** — the script is not open to this layer — so the reading above is the executor's. If the predecessor's was a per-component, or a degrees-of-freedom-corrected, RMS, then `S ≤ F` is stricter than its authors' by up to √3 (`EST-P-1`). **Contingency, written now so that it is not a post-hoc loosening:** if `S > F` and `S/√3 ≤ F`, the result is reported as "reaches only under the per-component reading" and **brought to the manager unchanged**; the executor does not call it a pass.
